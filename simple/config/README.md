# Proposed initial production settings

Prepared 2026-10-28 for the new Jupiter-only pooled program. The owner selected a **free Jupiter API key** and preparation of the production roles. No paid provider, public role assignment, funded operation, initialization or deployment is implied by these files. `production-*` is the primary proposal; `keyless-*` is a separate fallback requiring a different immutable policy and fresh database/history. Neither profile is activated.

## Financial terms selected for review

| Setting | Proposal | Reason and bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed term / grace | 24 hours / 24 hours | One day loan plus the owner's exact one day grace. No fee accrues during grace. At 48 hours from opening, default is the only economic resolution. |
| Upfront fee | 1% of opening notional | Paid outside collateral; 80% to current lenders and 20% to protocol. No early-close refund. This is a fixed charge for the loan, not an APR or guaranteed yield. |
| Opening / liquidation collateral ratio | 250% / 200% | If initial bid equals opening notional and collateral is unchanged, the strict bid-health boundary is a rise above 25%. Historic bad-price and later confirmation checks still apply. It is not a promise that a whole-token buyback remains possible. |
| Position notional | 0.1 to 1 SOL | Excludes tiny loans whose keeper/network overhead dominates. The capped notional is `max(actual sale, live ask upper, historical ask upper maximum)`, not token count. |
| Market booked notional | 5 SOL | At most five 1 SOL positions or fifty 0.1 SOL positions at one time under this policy, before wallet/utilization/availability constraints. This does not cap current replacement cost or aggregate protocol exposure. |
| Wallet share | 20% of market cap | 1 SOL aggregate booked notional per wallet per market. Multiple wallets bypass this concentration limit. |
| Utilization | 60% when opening | Prevents a new borrow exceeding 60% of tracked token assets. Later withdrawals can change the ratio; this is not a permanent 40% liquid cash reserve. |
| Keeper reward | 0.0001 SOL + 0.5% of basis; maximum 0.002 SOL | Actual spend is the forced-exit basis; collateral is the default basis. Also limited by available collateral. Temporary account deposits and failed attempts still require a funded payer. |
| Forced premium / maximum spread | 5% / 5% | Spending also reserves the entire 0.002 SOL keeper cap. A missing whole-debt route remains unavailable. A finite ask upper is one successfully tested budget, not fair value or a guarantee of the cheapest or future route. |
| History | Three completed 60 second epochs | At least 180 seconds of contiguous exact-q history; allow up to 240 seconds initial epoch alignment plus observation/clock latency. A history maximum delays early liquidation after a price move. |
| Freshness / confirmation | 30 seconds AND 100 slots; 10 seconds AND 10 slots | Actual dual clocks apply. These are independent limits, not a conversion based on an assumed fixed slot duration. Confirmation proves separated observations, not every price between them. |

Example with sale and both ask values equal to **0.1 SOL**: borrower adds **0.15 SOL margin + 0.001 SOL fee**, excluding account deposits and network fees. Collateral is **0.25 SOL**. Lenders immediately receive **0.0008 SOL**, protocol **0.0002 SOL**. A maturity buyback costing 0.1 SOL pays a **0.0006 SOL** keeper reward. Default instead pays a **0.00135 SOL** keeper reward, **0.24865 SOL** to lenders and **zero economic SOL** to the borrower. Loan/exposure account rent and untracked donations remain separately handled by the program.

At a bid equal to three times that opening notional, an ask at the allowed 5% spread costs **0.315 SOL**, exceeding the **0.248 SOL** collateral-based forced budget. Default's 0.24865 SOL is only **78.93%** of that tested replacement cost, before any later movement. This deliberately retained stress vector shows that these collateral settings do not guarantee full token recovery. The authenticated bid health test and the authenticated finite ask acquisition test have different purposes; neither makes the other a fair-market oracle. Manipulation, gaps, unavailable routes, operator failure and reporter dishonesty remain risks.

Deposits and permissionlessly created market count stay uncapped, and there is no global exposure cap. Eligible plain SPL or metadata-only Token-2022 mints still need an admitted executable route and service coverage; arbitrary listed tokens and arbitrary quantities are not promised instant borrowing. Old positions retain their original immutable terms after a policy update. The never-deployed legacy release, old fuzz duration and old external venue qualifications do not qualify this program.

## Free provider capacity and operating costs

`production-service.proposed.json` uses one HTTP request start per second, **one exact `(market, policy, quantity)` slot**, and a 15 second sampling target with a 30 second maximum gap. Distinct loans with the same tuple can share history; different quantities cannot. Four new-reference allocation slots are a separate lifetime rent allowance, not four simultaneous history slots. The prior-ask fast path requires two successful GETs in the ordinary no-fallback case, consuming two seconds of steady spacing and leaving thirteen seconds of the target for latency and other work.

Each quote first requests routing-account budget 16, then may try 32 only when the primary route is unavailable or outside the admitted header envelope. Each probe therefore has at most two HTTP starts. With one sale and at most two buy probes, a maximum-cost history observation consumes **six seconds** of spacing. An execution buy search with eight probes can add **sixteen seconds**, using **22 seconds combined before network/RPC/scheduler/transaction latency** against a 30 second maximum sample gap. This is the reason for commissioning one quantity initially. The capacity report intentionally remains unready until sustained end-to-end timings are measured. Neither a second quantity nor a larger queue is automatically qualified. Active loan coverage takes priority over new admission, and a full operator pass cannot overlap the next pass. Different clients or databases still share the provider allowance.

The separate keyless proposal uses two second spacing, one quantity, and a 30 second target / 60 second maximum gap, with 60 second AND 150 slot freshness. It is a reduced commissioning fallback, not an automatic live settings switch. Its maximum six-HTTP-start history observation takes twelve seconds of spacing; an execution buy search can add thirty-two seconds, using **44 seconds combined** against the 60 second maximum gap before other work. Both profiles use deterministic routing-account budgets **16 then 32**, admit at most 40 returned quote metas and 768 instruction-data bytes (the on-chain ceiling remains 48 metas), and explicitly exclude the currently incompatible Scorch family. This includes no new general DEX allowlist and does not qualify every Jupiter route family. The exact fallback/exclusion profile is part of each model hash. Both profiles use at most 4 SOL for a tested acquisition; that covers initial 2.5 SOL collateral at the 1 SOL position cap, but a later larger replacement cost can become unavailable. Extra margin does not automatically raise the service's budget.

The current [Jupiter provider documentation](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/portal/rate-limits) gives Free access 1 RPS and keyless access 0.5 RPS, with shared organization budgets. There is no subscription purchase in this proposal. Neither a free API account nor a public RPC endpoint provides measured availability for this application.

Local history sampling needs no on-chain transaction. Publish authenticated references on demand for opening or a keeper decision; do not publish every advancing healthy sample. At the proposed one quantity every 15 seconds, hypothetical continuous one-signature publication would cost **0.0288 SOL/day** in base fees alone, or **0.144 SOL/day** at the proposed 25,000-lamport per-transaction ceiling. This is a comparison explaining why healthy history remains off-chain, not the publication schedule or current spend. An illustrative two on-demand publications at that ceiling cost **0.00005 SOL per loan**, before all other costs; two is not a guaranteed upper bound on publications or keeper actions. Solana documents a current [5,000-lamport signature fee plus optional priority fees](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees); unsuccessful submitted transactions can also incur fees.

The minimum loan's protocol revenue is only **0.0002 SOL**. A 205-byte exact-q reference account is funded by the reporter, and this program has no reference-close instruction. The [dated read-only RPC receipt](../../docs/audit/simple-current-route-evidence-2026-10-28/reference-rent205.receipt.json) observed **1,691,640 lamports (0.00169164 SOL)** at **2026-10-28T19:57:48.450888Z** for 205 bytes. That is **nine minimum-loan gross protocol fee shares** before transaction/service costs; it is a stored-capital requirement, not a funding receipt or a guarantee of future storage prices. Its current RPC quote must be rechecked before operation; the operator allows at most **0.01 SOL per new reference**, which is **50 minimum-loan protocol fee shares**. Even a lower actual storage deposit can exceed that first loan's revenue. Reusing the same exact-q reference spreads that deposit over later loans; arbitrary new quantities and new markets do not. Budget this initial subsidy and retain unused account deposits as locked working capital. Do not claim that fees already fund operation, audit or bounty. The runner also proposes an allowance of **four new reference accounts per persistent operator database**, exposed as `--max-new-reference-accounts 4`; preserve the database and reconcile pending allocation outcomes rather than resetting this allowance to admit more quantities. Durable distinct-address reservations are made before submission; preparation failure or unknown submission keeps its reservation, and repeated use of the same address is idempotent. This bounds one initial reference-deposit subsidy to 0.04 SOL under the explicit 0.01 SOL per-account ceiling while that operator database is preserved. Public warm-up/rent admission, repeat quantities and actual operating costs must be commissioned before expanding coverage.

`operator-bounds.proposed.json` keeps execution false, limits each transaction to 25,000 lamports and each pass to 100,000, and allows up to 0.01 SOL temporary accounts or a new reference deposit. Suggested starting balance is 0.1 SOL per operating payer, with a 0.05 SOL minimum residual/low-balance threshold. These are proposed wallet budgets, not a funding receipt. Three 25,000-lamport transactions cost 0.000075 SOL versus the minimum loan's illustrative 0.0006 SOL maturity reward, but that comparison excludes repeated failures, priority pressure, service costs and locked deposits. Native route setup is a separate actor cost, with at most 0.005 SOL signed per routed instruction and unused allowance refunded. In the successful current [legacy Pump curve lifecycle](../../docs/audit/simple-current-route-evidence-2026-10-28/runtime/current-route-suite-8c4a-spread500/pump-curve-adaptive/replay/summary.json), opening setup consumed **0.00219936 SOL**; [graduated PumpSwap](../../docs/audit/simple-current-route-evidence-2026-10-28/runtime/current-route-suite-8c4a-spread500/pump-graduated-adaptive-440/replay/summary.json) and the [fresh Token-2022 Pump curve](../../docs/audit/simple-current-route-evidence-2026-10-28/runtime/current-route-suite-8c4a-spread500/pump-fresh-native-adaptive/replay/summary.json) each consumed **0.0018444 SOL**. The opening borrower funded that allowance separately. All three same-loan whole-debt buybacks succeeded with **zero native setup allowance and zero setup spend** in those captured lifecycles. The keeper therefore retains a zero native setup allowance for this proposal. This does not prove an arbitrary future alternate route needs no native setup. If a future keeper route needs 0.0018444 SOL, that exceeds the minimum loan's illustrative 0.0006 SOL reward; even the 0.002 SOL cap leaves only 0.0001556 SOL before network/service costs. A positive keeper allowance requires a funded net-cost/subsidy decision. It never charges lenders or borrower collateral, and the whole allowance may be consumed without proving every destination was rent. No keeper profit or execution guarantee is claimed. Repeated requests or an expiring unfillable loan can still request repeated publications across passes, so `--max-daily-publication-fees-lamports 1000000` proposes an additional **0.001 SOL per UTC day** publication-fee reservation budget in the persistent operator database. At a 25,000-lamport ceiling this funds at most 40 publications; at 5,000 lamports, at most 200. Once reserved before signing, signing failures and unknown submissions conservatively retain the fee allowance. A pre-simulation failure sends nothing and reserves no publication fee. This budget excludes one-time reference deposits and keeper transactions, and exhaustion must alert and stop additional publication; direct repayment and deterministic default stay independent. Preserve and test the daily budget across restart before activation. No automatic top-up or financial transaction is authorized by this manifest.

## Role assignment and checks

Follow the concrete [public-role and local-key setup guide](ROLE_SETUP.md).

`production-roles.template.json` contains **no production identities or credentials**. Prepare distinct governance, emergency and reporter roles; keep the operating keeper separate from governance where possible. Record public keys, actual mainnet genesis and the reviewed current Jupiter loader slot/length/ELF digest. The actual initializer must be authorized by canonical ProgramData upgrade authority. Generate or select keys privately on the target machine, protect their backups, and share only public keys here. Put local role keyfile paths in the runtime invocation after review; never put wallet bytes, seed phrases, RPC secrets or API keys in these JSON files. `SOLANA_RPC_URL` and `JUPITER_API_KEY` remain environment values.

The verifier accepts the incomplete manifest as a **non-executable proposal**; requiring assigned roles fails until every public identity and reviewed loader field is assigned. Assigned roles alone are not deployment approval or commissioned operation. `execute` remains false, and funding, alert delivery, measured service coverage, final route compatibility, final runtime review and fresh 24-hour evidence remain pending.

From `simple/`:

```sh
python3 config/verify-production-proposal.py --write-report config/production-sensitivity.proposed.json
python3 services/src/shortwin_price_service.py --settings config/production-service.proposed.json model-hash
python3 services/src/shortwin_price_service.py --settings config/production-service.proposed.json check-policy config/production-policy.proposed.json
python3 config/verify-production-proposal.py --require-assigned-roles
python3 -m unittest discover -s config -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

The last command is expected to fail while assignments remain empty. The sensitivity report uses checked unsigned integer amount arithmetic, retains exact rounding/stress/fee results and compares policy bindings through the real service checker. Any settings or price-service model change requires regenerating the proposal hash, a new immutable policy and a new database/fresh history. Do not silently repair deployed policy drift. The primary and fallback policy files are review inputs using decoded camelCase financial terms and hex hashes; they are not signed governance instructions or transaction plans.
