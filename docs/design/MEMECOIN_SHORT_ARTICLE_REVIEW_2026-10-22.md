# Memecoin shorting article review — 2026-10-22

## Decision and evidence

This review corrects an important description of the existing protocol: positions lock their **rent-curve coefficients**, while their APR continues to follow market utilization and their SOL rent follows the borrowed tokens' EMA-priced value. The article's proposal to introduce floating utilization rent therefore addresses behavior that already exists. The production protocol changes clarify that behavior; they change no runtime rule, fee, cap, default, account layout, ABI or UI. The patch also adds standalone executable host-validation artifacts and corrected publication copy.

The nine proposals identify useful research questions, but the article does not establish amounts or mechanisms that justify a runtime change. In particular, a funded first-loss buffer, paid observation market, withdrawal charge and unconditional settlement deadline each require a separate economic and control design. Leaving current rules unchanged is **not** a finding that their incentives are safe or optimal.

## Claims about current behavior

“Supported” below means supported by the checked source or a stated model result. It does not mean independently measured on mainnet or economically optimal. “Conditional” means the claim needs the specified assumptions; “unverified” means the supplied evidence does not reproduce it.

| Article claim | Assessment and correction |
|---|---|
| Rent splits 85/15, with an 80% kink and 90% utilization cap. | **Supported defaults.** The 90% limit is an opening borrowing constraint, not an APR cap or a guarantee that later withdrawals cannot raise utilization. [Params](../../crates/shortwin-math/src/params.rs), [caps](../../programs/shortwin/src/engine/caps.rs), ECONOMICS §7. |
| A position locks its rate, so older positions need floating rates above the kink. | **Incorrect premise.** Positions snapshot `base`, `slope1`, `slope2` and protocol share. The market integrates current utilization and EMA price into rent basis indices throughout the loan. Governance changing coefficients for new loans is a separate issue. [Rent math](../../crates/shortwin-math/src/rent.rs), [prologue](../../programs/shortwin/src/engine/prologue.rs), [position](../../programs/shortwin/src/state/position.rs). |
| Fixed expiry caps lender exposure at 72 hours on curves. | **Conditional.** Tier D's maximum term is 72 hours; its deadline makes it eligible for expiry, not guaranteed repayment at that instant. Route failure, reference bounds, warm-up and settlement evidence can delay resolution. The position's lock horizon also includes snapshotted migration/S3 timers. SPEC §4; ECONOMICS §5.6, §9.3, §10.3. |
| Tier D opens at 400% CR and liquidates at 175%. | **Supported defaults.** CR uses executable buyback cost and rent-adjusted vault value, rather than simply spot price times tokens. The EMA's five seconds is a time constant, not a simple five-print average. Liquidation persistence defaults to three updates; S1/S2 settlement persistence separately defaults to 40 updates. [Tier defaults](../../crates/shortwin-math/src/tier.rs); ECONOMICS §5. |
| Keepers receive 30% of the penalty plus a settlement reward. | **Misleading sum.** Normal liquidation pays the penalty share plus only the top-up needed to reach the settlement reward, limited by available borrower remainder. Settlement pays its own reward; it is not another full payment added to every liquidation. [Waterfalls](../../crates/shortwin-math/src/waterfall.rs). |
| S1 takes the vault less keeper reward; S2/S3 cap lenders at fair value. | **Supported distinction.** S1 is uncapped. S2/S3 use executable debt cost at the specified smoothed state, rent and the applicable penalty or expiry fee, bounded by available funds. They do not guarantee that lenders can repurchase every missing token at a future price. ECONOMICS §8.6. |
| Six-hour premium vesting prevents just-in-time capture. | **Supported for the specified atomic capture mechanism.** It is not insurance or a defense against every existing concentrated lender. Current deposit holds and rent top-up rules also matter. SPEC §2.4; ECONOMICS §9 and its candidate notice. |
| Raydium requires 50% of the whole LP supply locked, and PumpSwap 100%. | **Incorrect.** Raydium can admit lower locked fractions when the locked-SOL floors clear; its lock parameter is a sizing divisor. PumpSwap permits third-party withdrawable LP and sizes against burned depth `D`; a 100% divisor applied to `D` does not mean the whole pool is locked. ECONOMICS §1.2–1.3, §6.1–6.2. |
| Every settlement decision requires five attacker-owned seconds out of eight. | **Too broad.** Five attacker-only buckets are needed to move the protective low/high medians inward beyond honest observations. Contrary prints can instead widen uncertainty or veto another required condition and cause delay without that majority. Honest same-second observations protect specific extrema; they do not erase every manipulated observation. ECONOMICS §5.6. |
| Coverage failure “fails safe” and never blocks exits. | **Qualified statement.** The coverage pause only gates new opens. It does not remove existing borrowers' and lenders' exposure to missing observations, adverse prices or stale references, and it does not bypass normal exit preconditions. SPEC §11; ECONOMICS §5.8. |
| Four wallets defeat the 25% wallet cap. | **Supported address-limit warning.** The safety bound is market-wide OI. Address counts and concentration cannot establish how many independent actors exist. SPEC §12 R1. |
| Governance can only change bounded parameters after at least 24 hours. | **Incomplete.** The compiled program minimum is 24 hours and its default 48 hours. The documented Squads policy adds its own 48-hour timelock. Governance also retains upgrade authority, so parameter bounds do not describe every possible future governance power. SPEC §11. |

Default terms make the rate distinction concrete:

| Tier | Maximum term | APR at 80% utilization | APR at 90% utilization | Minimum rent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 30 days | 50% | 150% | 1 hour |
| B | 14 days | 100% | 250% | 1 hour |
| C | 7 days | 200% | 500% | 1 hour |
| D | 72 hours | 400% | 950% | 4 hours |

The minimum **amount** is fixed at open; continuing rent uses the changing inputs. Hypothetical 100%-utilization APRs in design tables are points on the rate curve beyond the opening utilization cap.

## Economic interpretations and numerical claims

| Article claim | Assessment and implication |
|---|---|
| “A lender is short a call.” | **Useful incremental-payoff analogy.** Relative to holding, capped cash recovery resembles forgone upside. The full position retains long token exposure plus SOL income and contingent conversion. Normal token repayment, early liquidation, partial utilization, pool accounting, vesting and rent shortfalls make it path dependent, unlike a standard fixed-strike call. |
| Idle holders' alternative is zero, so any rent beats nothing. | **Misleading benchmark.** Holding earns no rental cash but preserves token upside and liquidity. Positive rent alone does not establish a positive return relative to holding or compensate tail losses and additional SOL obligations. |
| Lending is a forward sale at a premium for someone wanting to exit. | **Incorrect as a general description.** Normal repayment returns tokens. It does not guarantee a SOL sale price or conversion date. A self-lend/self-short strategy requires consolidated wallet, margin, fee and inventory accounting. |
| Lenders expecting further rises prefer settlement to token repayment. | **Not established by that expectation.** Returned tokens retain future upside; fixed SOL does not. A settlement preference needs a premium, exit preference, different forecast, or an additional LP/inventory position to be specified. |
| An insider's lending necessarily transfers a loss to spot buyers. | **Causal claim needs evidence.** Borrowing sells tokens immediately, but the lender usually retains a token claim and shorters may buy back later. Compare the actor's consolidated strategy with a direct sale before attributing an external loss. Concentration alone cannot prove it. |
| Self-lending costs only 15% of rent and 20% of a penalty. | **Conditional ownership assumption.** Recovering 85% of rent assumes essentially all relevant lending exposure is owned. Paying only the protocol's 20% penalty share also assumes capturing the keeper's 30%; a lender/borrower using an external keeper pays that keeper too. |
| No points or volume rewards means wash trading earns nothing. | **Only removes that subsidy.** Signal manipulation, utilization griefing and price effects remain possible motives. Introducing insurance or observation rewards would create additional incentive questions. |
| Cushions are approximately 2.1×/2.6×/3.1×/4×; a 10× gap loses 58–78%. | **Source-model examples, not guarantees.** ECONOMICS §11 uses specified venues, sizes, fees, position age and keeper timing. The 58–78% denominator is **terminal lent-token value**. It is neither opening notional nor the entire pool portfolio. |
| Income is about 5% per full-term position; Tier D breaks even at a gap every four weeks. | **Calibration-sensitive.** The source's 4.79–5.49% examples include full-term assumptions and a 2% expiry fee that ordinary borrower closure does not pay. The approximately 28-day D break-even uses `alpha = 2`; other illustrated tails differ substantially. Preserve the model's jump-arrival definition rather than treating it as an observed conditional frequency of cushion-breaking gaps. |
| Calm Tier C returns approximately −13.6%; stressed outcomes can exceed −100%. | **Illustrative, uncalibrated model results.** The former is approximately −13.57% ± 8.62% of **opening notional** over 400 paths. Missing tokens can appreciate beyond opening notional, so loss versus holding can exceed 100% of that denominator without proving cash outflow exceeding the original deposit. ECONOMICS §11.6. |
| Standard curve completion from 80% progress gives 2.46×; migration always adds 45.66% within six minutes. | **Conditional arithmetic, overgeneralized forecast.** The standard exact-completion reserves support approximately 2.46× and a standard boost supports `(1 + 0.2069)^2 ≈ 1.4566`. Current venue notes describe seed changes after synthetic overshoots/donations and the general boost formula. Other trades and future venue changes affect the realized path; six minutes is not a short.win guarantee. ECONOMICS §1.2, §10.1. |
| Graduation is an unpaid option; the worst scenario loses about 35%. | **Risk supported, pricing inference unproven.** The exact-completion D@50% example loses 34.7% versus terminal holding; examples with subsequent AMM rises lose more. D@80/90% examples return tokens through normal liquidation, and D@97% can survive completion. Ordinary rent already pays for some risk; progress alone does not determine a correct extra premium or a monotone risk schedule. |
| The existing graduation guard and boost/migration gates solve part of this risk. | **Supported with exact conditions.** The 50% guard uses conservative reserve inputs. The boost gate uses a 0.05 SOL dust threshold within 900 seconds. Default migration pause, graduation grace and S3 wait are 3,600/600/600 seconds. None is an unconditional repayment guarantee. |
| Held manipulation loses a Tier A borrower about 19.5 SOL and lenders/protocol 3.3 SOL. | **Recorded no-arbitrage model example.** The result depends on a held reference and keeper/arbitrage assumptions. That example's approximately 60.8 SOL opening notional exceeds the 25 SOL beta guard; it must not be represented as a permitted 25 SOL beta position or a universal loss bound. ECONOMICS §4, §12.6. |
| A 2% exit bound limits sandwich gains to about 1.5 SOL, always from the borrower. | **Incorrect as an overall bound.** It bounds spending relative to a reference that a held path can lift. The same source records larger held gains and possible reductions in lender/protocol penalty or rent. A local atomic/fixed-reference example does not bound every strategy. |
| Curve shorting costs approximately 8% over 72 hours at 80% utilization. | **Reasonable conditional approximation.** Unchanged notional gives approximately 3.29% rent, 2.5% venue round-trip fees, 2% expiry and 0.25% opening fee, before keeper/transaction costs. At 90% utilization the analogous total is approximately 12.56%. Changing prices, utilization and early closure change the result. |
| Separate toy-model means, top-5% outcomes, push capital/fees, 10% attack return and 4.5× cap effect. | **Unreproduced.** The supplied article does not identify enough exact toy-model inputs, version, seeds and accounting to validate these figures from its text. They remain research hypotheses and cannot calibrate a production cap or premium. |

The public-goods concern is substantive: honest observations benefit all exposed users, while no current bounty pays for each observation. Funding an observer does not establish that its observations will land or be economically honest. Recorded modeled coverage costs, based on historical compute measurements, vary with batching, priority price, required duty cycle and market count; this review made no fresh coverage or landing measurements. The protocol's 15% rent share is also a proposed source for insurance, so both uses compete for the same revenue. Universal self-funding cannot be inferred for arbitrarily small markets.

The [CFTC's Mango release](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8647-23) supports the historical oracle-manipulation analogy, not an equivalence between inflated cross-asset collateral withdrawals and ShortWin's isolated short positions. The article names no specific academic paper for its liquidation-cost claim, so that precise attribution is unverified here.

[Shortr's current public docs](https://shortr.fun/docs) describe open-ended rolled shorts and time-locked lending buckets, but also an insurance fund that compensates lender bad debt before lenders absorb residual losses. That omitted layer matters to the comparison. These are documented public design claims, **not independently verified deployed behavior**. Changing a shortfall's denomination from tokens to SOL does not itself reduce its economic value when both are measured consistently.

## Expiry rewards: a minimum already exists

The current pre-truncation expiry reward is `min(0.001 SOL + 0.1% × B_ref, 0.05 SOL)`. It therefore already has a fixed component; what it lacks is a floor matching `K_s = min(0.01 SOL + 1% × V, 0.25 SOL, V)`. Expiry payment is further limited to the remainder after buyback, rent and the lender expiry fee. Normal liquidation's `K_s` top-up is also remainder-limited. Neither is an unconditional externally funded guarantee. See [risk.rs](../../crates/shortwin-math/src/risk.rs) and [waterfall.rs](../../crates/shortwin-math/src/waterfall.rs).

At `B_ref = 0.1 SOL`, the existing nominal expiry reward is **1,100,000 lamports**. ECONOMICS §5.8's recorded modeled **one-liquidation episode** at 100,000 micro-lamports/CU costs approximately **1,030,000 lamports**, including an assumed 1,000,000-lamport tip; this is not a measured expiry-episode cost. That table uses historical compute measurements with a requested limit derived as `ceil(consumed × 1.1)`; it does not simply bill consumed CU. Expiry has separate compute inputs, lower than the corresponding liquidation in those measurements. The comparison suggests narrow slack under those assumptions, not proof of underpayment or future congestion coverage.

Official [Solana fee documentation](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees) specifies a base fee of 5,000 lamports per signature. For the **CU-priced priority-fee form**, the fee depends on the requested CU limit and CU price, rather than consumed CU; v1 instead carries an absolute priority fee in lamports. Cost estimates must use the chosen transaction's fee form. Tips, extra signatures, retries, failed attempts, multi-transaction episodes and congestion-dependent landing conditions must be budgeted separately; the chain does not guarantee the modeled fee or latency.

Raising the fixed component from 1,000,000 to 10,000,000 lamports adds **9,000,000 lamports**, or **9% of the 0.1 SOL minimum notional** before any other costs. Compiled parameter ceilings are not recommended defaults. A fee or seniority change needs evidence about keeper opportunity cost, landing success and the borrower's extra cost, together with a defined funding source if payment is to be guaranteed.

## All nine proposals

| # | Proposal and current decision | Evidence or design required before changing behavior |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Progress-dependent curve caps/rent: defer.** Completion risk is credible; no progress premium or schedule is established. | Compare exact and synthetic completion, post-migration stress, aged rent, completion capital and completion frequency across progress. Severity and probability need not increase together. Specify effects on new versus existing positions and borrower demand; do not call an invented premium incentive-compatible. |
| 2 | **First-loss buffer from the protocol share: defer.** It can transfer funded losses, but its adequacy and net benefit are unproven. | Define initial capital, replenishment, capped claims, market isolation, beneficiary priority and self-borrow/default resistance. At unchanged D notional and 80% utilization, the entire 15% share of 72-hour rent is only approximately 0.49% of notional, before operating costs. Preserve explicit residual lender loss and avoid unfunded insurance promises. |
| 3 | **Paid observation market: defer; retain explicit operating-budget disclosure.** The funding problem is real. | Define what delivery is rewarded, how truth is assessed, who pays, reward caps, redundant-print/Sybil/self-trigger defenses and what happens when funding expires. Payment for a print is not proof that the print is honest. A new payment authority or escrow needs narrow control and isolation rules. |
| 4 | **Tighter curve OI caps: defer numeric changes.** Smaller exposure can be a prudent owner choice, but no profitable-attack threshold is validated here. | Reconcile the toy attack with the current model, fee views, beta cap, wallet allowance, minimum notional, keeper timing and arbitrage. Tightening can make existing minimum-sized loans unavailable at some curve states and reduce fee funding. Preserve admission-only semantics and avoid unrelated-market effects. No arbitrary factor is adopted. |
| 5 | **Utilization withdrawal fee for stayers: defer.** A run externality does not prove this charge is the best remedy. | Compare cohort outcomes with existing locks, vest forfeiture, late-depositor liabilities and partial withdrawals. Test whether it encourages earlier exits, rewards concentrated stayers or makes stressed liquidity more procyclical. The reported 4–11% runner premium is model-specific, not a universal fee schedule. |
| 6 | **Floating above-kink rent for old positions: reject as redundant; clarify documentation.** | Existing utilization response already applies to open positions. A different coefficient-governance policy would be a separate proposal with borrower compatibility and stress-CR effects, not this factual correction. |
| 7 | **Expiry floor mirroring liquidation: defer amount/mechanism.** Existing fixed minimum differs from a `K_s`-aligned floor. | Measure fee limits, retries, timing, opportunity cost and incentives to wait for another exit type. Prefer existing snapshotted terms if sufficient; preserve payout conservation and explain borrower costs. A guaranteed floor requires its own funded or senior claim. Current modeled costs do not establish a necessary default increase. |
| 8 | **Wallet concentration disclosure: useful, deferred to a scoped UI/data change.** No concentration feature is implemented in this patch. | Normalize economic ownership across share epochs/rebases; require a complete dataset or label incompleteness. Show top-wallet shares and observation time, not inferred beneficial owners or “insiders.” Existing wallet limits do not defeat Sybils. |
| 9 | **Hard settlement deadline “whatever contrary prints”: reject the unconditional rule.** | Preserve current genuine recovery/refutation, eligibility, reference and payout evidence. An attacker could target a known deadline after conditions recover; conflicting observations can be honest. Any terminal auction, independent price or other fallback is a new allocation mechanism requiring separate design and validation. Time alone does not make an unsupported settlement safe. |

For proposal 9, the existing arming clock already survives mere spot-leg vetoes within its continuity rules. Current robust settlement conditions and amount checks still apply when settlement executes. Removing those checks would exchange a known deferral problem for a predictable forced-transfer opportunity.

## Remaining evidence and compatibility limits

The historical gap and Monte Carlo tables are illustrative and calibration-sensitive. ECONOMICS §11.6 describes pending public-RPC calibration with at least 30 days of tapes from October 2; no fitted replacement distribution or fresh simulation result is invented here. A positive quoted APR, an intentional spec and an internally consistent payout identity are each insufficient to prove positive expected lender returns.

Future work must use a common terminal benchmark and distinguish opening notional, terminal token value, the borrowed fraction and total pool equity. It must also include the documented possibility that a late lender shares a prior accrued-rent shortfall and can need additional SOL to withdraw. The covered-call analogy alone omits that liability.

The production protocol retains existing borrower/lender terms, keeper rewards, governance controls and transaction interfaces. Its documentation/comment clarification and the separate standalone validation additions do not alter production runtime behavior, defaults or ABI. This limited compatibility claim is not a general approval of the protocol's economic risks; the overall patch includes executable host-validation source and manifests as well as prose.

### Observed host results

One Tier D math position retained the same coefficients and minimum-rent amount while utilization changed from 8,000 to 9,000 bps at a constant EMA price. Its APR changed from 400% to 950%. Rent due after the first 24 hours was **10,958,905 lamports**; after another 24 hours at the higher utilization, cumulative rent was **36,986,302 lamports**. Changing a separate tier value did not alter the existing math-position value. This checks the production library's value-copy and accrual behavior, not persisted on-chain accounts or governance execution.

The expiry check used a 1 SOL buyback/reference cost, 0.01 SOL rent and 0.02 SOL lender expiry fee. It compared the current fixed component of 1,000,000 lamports with an explicit **what-if** fixed component of 10,000,000 lamports; the latter is not a selected default. All amounts below are lamports after those senior payments:

| Available remainder | Current keeper / borrower | What-if keeper / borrower |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 970,000,000 | 2,000,000 / 968,000,000 | 11,000,000 / 959,000,000 |
| 5,000,000 | 2,000,000 / 3,000,000 | 5,000,000 / 0 |
| 1,000,000 | 1,000,000 / 0 | 1,000,000 / 0 |
| 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |

All eight payouts conserved the declared vault and preserved the declared buyback, rent and lender expiry payments. The low- and zero-remainder cases show why a nominal reward minimum is not a guaranteed funded payout. They do not establish a desirable reward level, congestion coverage or keeper participation.

No SBF program, venue CPI, deployed state, on-chain transaction, live coverage/landing condition or tail-frequency calibration was tested. Persisted position snapshots and governance compatibility remain source-traced. The historical attack and Monte Carlo tables were not re-run. The wording-stage `git diff --check` passed; final aggregate whitespace checking and final commit review are separate pre-commit steps whose results are not claimed here.
