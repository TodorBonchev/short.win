Title: Curve quotes retain the original supply for fee tiers
seam: boundary×invariant
code_smells: The normal curve resolver uses PUMP_CURVE_MCAP_SUPPLY instead of the mint's live supply. A burn can move the live market cap across a fee threshold.
description: The curve resolver can choose the wrong fee after a supply burn if the venue adds multiple fee tiers; the present schedule prevents this.
path: A token holder burns circulating tokens → a future multi-tier schedule separates the fixed and live market caps → pump_curve selects the fixed-supply tier → normal curve quotes use that fee.
proof: fees.rs defines PUMP_CURVE_MCAP_SUPPLY = 1_000_000_000_000_000 and passes it to curve_fees_at. That helper computes floor(vq * supply / vt), then selects the last tier whose threshold is at most the result. For vt = 300_000_000_000_000, vq = 100_000_000_000, and live supply = 600_000_000_000_000, the fixed market cap is 333_333_333_333 lamports and the live market cap is 200_000_000_000. A hypothetical threshold of 250_000_000_000 separates them. If the lower tier has a 125 bps protocol fee and the higher tier has 100 bps, the normal resolver returns 100 bps while the live-supply resolver returns 125 bps. For q = 1_000_000_000_000, the normal curve quote has net = ceil(q * vq / (vt - q)) + 1 = 334_448_162. Its cost is 337_792_644 at 100 bps and 338_628_765 at 125 bps, a difference of 836_121 lamports. This is a pure integer calculation, not execution evidence.
caller_evidence: pricing_inputs receives supply but calls fees::pump_curve for its ordinary fees and its EMA fee. It separately calls pump_curve_at_supply for synthetic_fees. curve_synthetic retains ordinary curve_fees when q <= state.rt. Thus the existing synthetic correction does not change the ordinary branch.
guards_and_limitations: fees.rs:46-52 explicitly states that the current curve schedule has one tier at threshold zero. That condition makes the two market-cap bases select the same tier today. Owner-checked external fee data controls the schedule. This review did not inspect live accounts or prove an active multi-tier deployment. The open's min_notional, margin and fee gates do not select the missing supply basis, but they do prevent tiny notional and insufficient margin inputs. A trade CPI can reject an inadequate maximum input. This item remains a hardening lead, not an active exploit finding.
impact: A future fee threshold can cause a normal quote to differ from the venue charge after a burn. The difference can affect trade bounds and risk values that use that quote.
fix: Pass the verified live supply to normal curve fee resolution and use the same basis for the EMA fee.
confidence: high that the bases differ in code; conditional reachability because the source states one current tier.
related_scope: The adapter call is supporting evidence for this shared-library cause. This block does not assert a separate synthetic-fee gate defect.
